Ask a commercial image editor to preview a cosmetic procedure and it will often
change more of the face than the request names: a nose edit can also smooth
skin, alter lighting, or shift other features. Existing methods for confining
an edit to one region all require access to the model's internals, and a
service reached only through its public API exposes none of them. We therefore
ask how much control is possible from the client side alone. In a pilot
benchmark, six configurations of commercial editing models and one mask-based
inpainting endpoint perform facelift-style jaw--neck edits
and rhinoplasty edits at three increasing levels of client-side control:
sending the prompt alone, cutting the edited region out of the response and
pasting it back onto the original photograph through a landmark-derived mask
(a masked composite), and asking the endpoint itself to inpaint inside the
mask where that option exists. Of \NumAttemptedEdits{} attempted edits,
\NumScoredEdits{} could be scored. ArcFace cosine similarity measures how well
each output preserves the person's identity, and a CIELAB pixel-change ratio
measures how much of the change lands inside the requested region rather than
in a protected facial zone. On the \NumLocalizationFaces{} frontal faces the
regional metric could score, the masked composite improved this localization
score over the paired prompt-only output by a median of \MedPairedLocGain{}
(95\% face-clustered bootstrap interval
\PairedLocGainLow--\PairedLocGainHigh) while changing the requested region
about as much. The editors differed visibly in how strongly they edit versus
how well they preserve identity, and the one inpainting endpoint we tested did
not beat the simple composite. This is a study of control, not of clinical
accuracy: no surgeons rated the outputs, and each condition was generated
once. Within that scope, the practical finding is that keeping a surgical
preview inside its intended region needs no access to the model; a mask and
composite on the client enforce it across every editor we tested, at low
provider cost.
